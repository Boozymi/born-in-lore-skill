# The script formula – "What if you were born as …?"

Measured on all six Born in Lore videos (captions with word timestamps, chapter marks, descriptions). Sentence counts come from
the caption punctuation; jokes, plants and chapter types were classified by hand. Only short formula phrases are quoted –
everything else is described in our own words. The numbers per video: `study/transcripts.md`.

Constant in all six: the narrator starts at 0.0–0.8 s (no intro, no channel name), and after the final question 11–18 s of end
card run without a voice. The first video (Geralt) had no hook and had 12K views at the time of the study; two days later Kratos
opened with the hook below and had 823K (a week later: 35K against 1.24M) – every video since uses it.

## 1. The hook (0:00 – ~0:15)
1. `If you were born as [NAME], you'd [deed 1], then [deed 2], then [deed 3].` Conditional; concrete deeds you can picture at
   once; each one bigger than the last.
2. One **turn sentence**, usually starting with "And …": the origin ("it all starts with one very bad deal"), a betrayal,
   the death, or a moral twist (he lets the man live). It opens a question only the video answers.
3. The **hand-over**: `But right now, you're a baby [place], and [one trait or a list of what you don't have yet].` This is
   already the first line of the prologue (hook and prologue are one chapter, starting at 0:00).
- 32–50 words, 11–16 s, 2 sentences. The tense jump (you'd → present) marks the cut from the end of the life to the birth.
- **The baby sentence is the anchor of the ending:** its trait or its last listed item comes back in "Not bad for a …".
  Often the last item of the list is the symbol object of the whole video.

## 2. The prologue (birth to adulthood, 1–2.5 min, ~250 words)
Fixed order (5/5): birth + place + core trait → one sentence per parent (one is missing, dies or has a secret) → a sibling or a
substitute family → one childhood scene with an age ("At 13, …") that shows the core trait → the **first break** (someone is
taken or dies) → `Years pass, and the cute kid? Gone.` (5/6, write your own version) → a snapshot of the adult (three traits,
the last one a gag) → the people you are going to lose (a plant: "Remember that name.").
- The prologue is the backstory before the famous part – lore most viewers don't know.
- Shortest sentences of the video (Kratos: 7.7 words) and the funniest part (Kratos: 3.7 jokes per minute).
- 0–2 flash-forwards with "you'll", almost only here (a childhood detail with a lifelong effect).

## 3. The chapter engine
- **One chapter = one phase of life around one iconic moment**, 3–6 events; the iconic one gets most of the words, the others
  one bridge sentence each. Scene chapters (one moment almost in real time) and montage chapters ("You … You … You even …").
- **Titles:** 1–4 words – an object, a role or a place with "The" (22 of 52), or a famous line.
- **Openings (52 chapters counted):** never a recap. Direct action "You …/So you …" 29 %, "Then …" 27 %, a time marker 17 %,
  a place 17 %, a thesis sentence 10 %.
- **Endings = a button of 2–6 words**, never neutral information: a reveal, a new title or nickname ("Congratulations, you're
  the new …"), a two-word feeling ("You're home."), a cliffhanger, a number as a punch, a punchline.
- **Time jumps** always at the start of a sentence, often as a fragment ("8 years later."): ages, years, or "Years pass".
  The best video needs only 5 time markers.
- **Comment call always in chapter II, at 29–44 %**, 6–10 s, right after a punchline: "Quick pause." + who should be born
  next + the most liked comment wins. Then the story goes on with "Then …".

## 4. The voice
- Second person singular, **present tense**. Hook: "you'd". Life: present. A few "you'll". After "Not bad for": past tense.
  Final question: "would you".
- Mean 8.7–9.9 words per sentence, 27–37 % of sentences with ≤ 5 words, 4–9 % with ≥ 20. Rhythm: a set-up sentence of 12–18
  words, then a punch of 1–4 words ("Gone.", "You let him."). ~13 % of sentences start with Then/And then/So.
- Lore becomes something that happens to *you* (you climb for three days, the blades are burned into your arms). Feelings
  show through actions; they are named only at the turning point ("for the first time in your life …").
- New people get one label: `[Name], a [role] who [quirk].`
- Repeated devices: `Remember that.` / `Hold that thought.` as plants (2–4 per video) and `Remember …?` as the payoff
  question; `This time …` exactly twice, on the repeated situation with the outcome flipped; `Long story.` to skip whole arcs;
  self-answered questions ("Your answer? …") 4–8 per video; `Not a [X], a [Y].`
- **Jokes:** about 2.3 per minute (prologue ~3.7): deadpan after a hard fact, understatement, modern tone in a myth, a list of
  three with the gag last, a callback as the punchline. **No jokes at the low points and in the last 45–60 s.** The video with
  the most jokes (Geralt, 3.4 per minute, evenly spread) is the weakest – jokes alone don't carry it.

## 5. The arc and the ending
1. A funny childhood with planted seeds. 2. The first big catastrophe at 14–33 %. 3. Rise and set pieces with jokes.
4. A twist or betrayal in the middle, 43–60 %. 5. The loss of the most important person or of your own life at 74–92 %.
6. A quiet last image. The payoffs of the plants land at 54–74 % – exactly where viewers usually drop off.
- The last chapter is aftermath and legacy: the symbol objects come back, a title gets a new meaning, the action ends in a
  quiet picture. 45–111 s long.
- **The ending, 3 sentences, 11–14 s:**
  1. `Not bad for a [the baby image from the hook].`
  2. `You [greatest deed], [greatest loss], and still [human choice].` – past tense, three beats.
  3. `So, tell me, would you still [the decision the life hangs on]?` – the same question goes into the description.

## 6. Packaging
- **Title:** `What If You Were Born as [Name]? | [World]`. No tags.
- **Description, in this order:** "You're a baby [place]. …" + 1–3 sentences preview → `This is the whole life of [Name], from
  [first image] to [last image], told as if it were yours.` → 8–11 key moments, the last one a feeling ("and, finally, …") →
  the final question + "And who should be born next? Tell me in the comments." → the chapter list → a channel line with
  your own channel name and your own tagline (theirs ends in a "subscribe for the next life" line – write your own) → sources + "All illustrations, animation and music are original." + a fan-work disclaimer
  if needed → 3 hashtags.
- **Thumbnail:** the baby version ("AGE 0", in a crib) next to the grown-up version, one big word for what they became.
  The newest three have no text at all: only the baby next to the grown-up.

## 7. Why Kratos is the best one
Most compressed (8:06, 1,199 words, slowest pace 2.53 words/s, "Long story." instead of quotes – only 4 speech verbs), the
cleanest 32-word hook whose turn ("one very bad deal") is chapter I and the final question at once, 3 plants with 3 payoffs at
54 / 60 / 74 %, short even chapters (median 39 s, a new title every 40 s), almost no lore numbers, the funniest prologue and
joke-free low points, and a 12-second ending.

## 7b. Why Ghost is catchy (the biggest hit, read sentence by sentence)
Ghost has almost no jokes. It holds people with pictures, riddles and reversals:
- **The hook deeds are shocking pictures, not a résumé.** Buried alive, digging out with a dead man's jawbone, faking your death
  in a burning house. Each one is its own short "Then …" sentence.
- **The turn is a riddle about who ends you.** The episode answers it with the same words at the death scene: the man you will
  one day call "sir" is the general who shoots you.
- **The last item of the baby sentence is the dark theme of the life** (the scariest thing in your house is your dad).
- **One plant is a sound or a gesture,** here the father's laugh. It comes back three times: forced on you as a kid, out of
  you at the worst moment, and the last time flipped ("this time, you fight the laugh").
- **Scenes, not summaries.** Every chapter is one concrete action with a number you can picture (thirteen hours of digging,
  a month on foot to Texas) and a reversal in one sentence (the only man who came back from the enemy came back working for him).
- **1–3 short lines in the characters' own voice per chapter,** told or quoted, never a long speech.
- **Buttons are title drops** (the chapter "What Was Left" ends on what was left: a ghost).
- **The comment call talks to the comments** (the viewers asked for this character, so here it is).
- **The ending is a montage of the whole life** in short past-tense sentences, then a question the source never answers.

## 8. The template for a life of your own
No fixed length: the story decides. The channel runs 6:52–14:17 (1,049–2,197 words), the newest episodes 13–14 minutes.
Words ≈ seconds × 2.6; prologue + I–IX at every length. An original or public-domain character nobody owns:
the deeds in the hook must work without prior knowledge, and you need 2–3 repeated lines of your own instead of fan quotes,
plus one symbol object that appears 4 times.

```
GROUNDWORK
  CORE TRAIT (1 word)      : ___   (angry / wants to win / wants to be remembered …)
  SYMBOL OBJECT            : ___   (baby sentence → 2× in the middle → "Not bad for")
  THE DECISION             : ___   (chapter I; becomes the final question)
  3–4 PLANTS (prologue)    : a secret ___ / an object of a parent ___ / a lesson or name ___
  PAYOFFS (at 50–75 %)     : ___ / ___ / ___
  REPEATED LINE            : ___   (comes back 2–3×, the last time with "This time")
  TITLE THAT TURNS         : ___   (a nickname from I/II gets a new meaning at the end)

HOOK 0:00–0:13 (30–38 words, 2 sentences)
  If you were born as [NAME], you'd [deed 1], then [bigger deed 2], then [biggest deed 3].
  And [turn: origin | betrayal | death "Not a ___, a ___." | mercy].
  But right now, you're a baby [place], and you're already [CORE TRAIT].

PROLOGUE 0:13–1:55 (~250 words, short sentences, ~3.5 jokes/min)
  parents one sentence each (plant 1: "Remember that.")
  sibling/friend with a quirk (plant 2)
  "At [age], …" childhood scene showing the CORE TRAIT, a list of three with the gag last
  the first break → a symbolic act ("Not a ___, a promise.")
  your own "years pass" line → the adult in 3 traits, the last one a gag
  the people you will lose (plant 3: "Remember that name.")

CHAPTERS I–IX (as long as the moment needs: 30–60 s in the 8-minute videos, 35–180 s in the 13–14-minute ones; titles of 1–4 words)
  start: "Then …" | an age | a place | a direct consequence – never a recap
  body: 2–4 bridge sentences + 1 core scene in the present (max 1–2 short quoted lines) + 1 deadpan line (not at low points)
  button (2–6 words): reveal | new title | feeling | cliffhanger
  I    the decision + the first guilt                 → button: a nickname
  II   consequences and rise; comment call at ~38 %   → button: an ironic congratulation
  III  payoff of plant 2 (twist, short, ~35 s)
  IV   payoff of plant 1 (twist, shortest)
  V    the biggest set piece → loss, low point, no jokes
  VI   "Many years later" / a new phase; payoff of plant 3
  VII  a journey with someone new → a prophecy cliffhanger
  VIII the final confrontation; letting go
  IX   facing the past, the title that turns, a quiet last image

ENDING (last 12 s, then 12 s of music without a voice)
  Not bad for a [baby image].
  You [greatest deed], [greatest loss], and still [human choice].      ← past tense
  So, tell me, would you still [THE DECISION]?
  – or the newest form (Ghost, Marston, Tommy): 2–4 short past-tense lines about the life →
  So tell me, [THE DECISION as a question / a question the source never answers]? → Who should be born next? Tell me in the comments.

VOICE CHECK (tools/script_check.py)
  2nd person · present · "you'd" only in the hook · "you'll" 1–2× in the prologue · past tense only at the end
  mean ~9 words per sentence, ≥ 30 % with ≤ 5 words, none over 25 · ~13 % start with Then/So
  ~2 jokes/min (prologue ~3.5) · 0 jokes at the low point and in the last 45–60 s
  "This time" at the repetitions (2× in the first six, 1× in the newest) · "Long story." to skip · new person = "[Name], a [role] who [quirk]."
  at most ~5 numbers and ≤ 6 time markers
```

The Achilles episode follows this template line by line: `example/achilles/LIFE.md` (the filled-in groundwork and the beat
plan with timings and sources) and `example/achilles/voice/*.txt` (the finished script).
