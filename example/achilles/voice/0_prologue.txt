Your mom is Thetis, a sea goddess, and she wants you immortal. Hence the river. Your dad is Peleus, a mortal king, who was not consulted. Everything goes under, except the heel in her hand. Tiny detail. Then she tries a second trick, at night, holding you in the fire. Your dad walks in and screams. Your mom walks back into the sea. And that's your parents' marriage. Oh, and at their wedding, the one goddess nobody invited, Strife, rolled in a golden apple that says, for the fairest. Three goddesses are still fighting over it. Remember that apple. So your dad hands you to Chiron, a centaur. Half horse, half the best teacher in Greece. Breakfast is lion guts and bear marrow. At six, you drag home dead lions and wild boars, and you run down deer without dogs. Chiron teaches you to fight, to heal, and to play the lyre. The lyre, you think, is useless. Hold that thought. Back home, a new boy moves in. Patroclus. He killed another kid over a game of dice. Your dad's reaction? Perfect. New best friend. He's a year older, and the only one who can calm you down. In the hall hangs your dad's armor, a wedding gift from the gods. Remember that armor. And your mom? She knows exactly how your life ends. She just isn't saying. Then you grow up. Fast. Blond, proud, the fastest man in Greece. And still screaming for attention.
