The war goes on. You beat the queen of the Amazons, and a son of the Dawn. Then, at the gates of Troy, an arrow flies. It comes from Paris. Remember that apple? Paris is the one who picked who got it. That choice started this war. Apollo guides his arrow. Into your heel. The one spot the river never touched. The fastest man alive, stopped by his own foot. Your ashes go into a golden jar, together with the ashes of Patroclus. And down in the land of the dead, Odysseus finds you. One last time. He says you're lucky. You rule down here. You tell him, better a poor man's servant, alive, than king of all the dead. Then you ask about your son, the one you left on that island. He's brave, Odysseus says. Just like you. And you walk away across a field of grey flowers. Happy. Almost three thousand years later, we still know your name. Not bad for a baby hanging by one heel. You killed Troy's greatest prince, lost your best friend, and still gave a father back his son. So, tell me, would you still choose the short life?
